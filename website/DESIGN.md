# Design and content decisions
Temporary brand: Clearstead. Name, contact details, geography and actual service availability need confirmation before public launch. No invented clients, credentials, testimonials or financial results.

Inventory: monthly books; reconciliations; pay cycles; online-store settlements; owner decisions; tailored scope; secure onboarding; quote-based pricing; optional payroll intake; enquiry-first scheduling.

Argument: show what support is offered; let the visitor find a service; explain engagement steps; connect industries to services; explain payroll scope; answer practical questions; receive a real consultation request.

Idea: the brief's promise of clear accounting becomes a large two-line typographic statement, followed by a working service selector. This remains a category concept until real company identity is supplied.

Direction revised after the user's feedback that the site was too text-heavy: photography-led editorial composition. Retain the brief palette and Manrope/Inter. The hero pairs the promise with a prominent financial-review photograph; a bookkeeping story and document-detail images make the work tangible throughout the site. Use six service cards, subtle borders, 12px buttons and accessible FAQ accordion as requested in the original brief. No fabricated dashboard or results. All stock photos are illustrative, not the firm's people or premises; credits appear on /credits.

Latest user correction: service cards retain the original line icons, without photography. Remove the added bookkeeping-story section and repeated process images. Photography belongs in the main hero and the dedicated About, Services and service-detail page content; retain the original industry images.

Copy deck: Accounting made clear. Bookkeeping, payroll and reporting support for businesses ready for their next chapter. Support that adds up. Find your starting point. A clear way forward. Built around your business. More time for your team. Good questions. Let's talk numbers.

Implementation: React.js/Vite frontend and standalone Node.js backend. No management dashboard or client portal. Node runtime is intentionally retained rather than converting the backend to a hosted Worker.
